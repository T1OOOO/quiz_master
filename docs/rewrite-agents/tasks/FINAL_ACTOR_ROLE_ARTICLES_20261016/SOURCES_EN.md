## 039 — *Thelma & Louise* (1991): Susan Sarandon — Louise Sawyer

1. [AFI Catalog: *Thelma & Louise* (1991)](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/59064) — AFI credits Susan Sarandon as Louise Elizabeth Sawyer, Geena Davis as Thelma Dickinson, and Harvey Keitel as Hal Slocumbe. It credits Callie Khouri as writer, Ridley Scott as director, Adrian Biddle as cinematographer, Thom Noble as editor, and Hans Zimmer for music. AFI records the 1990 casting of Davis and Sarandon, and notes that Meryl Streep and Goldie Hawn had earlier been contenders for the roles.
2. [Academy: The 64th Academy Awards (1992)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1992) — the Academy film-summary header reports five grouped nomination categories and one win, but its category roster contains six individual nomination entries because Davis and Sarandon are separately listed in Actress in a Leading Role. Khouri won Original Screenplay; Biddle, Scott, and Noble received the other individual nominations.

**Distractor and source caution:** Davis is Thelma and Keitel is Hal; Streep was considered before casting but does not appear in the final film. Louise and the road-film events are fictional. Keep the 1991 release and the 1992 Academy ceremony separate.

## 040 — *One Flew Over the Cuckoo’s Nest* (1975): Jack Nicholson — Randle Patrick McMurphy

1. [AFI Catalog: *One Flew Over the Cuckoo’s Nest* (1975)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/55189-One-Flew-Over-the-Cuckoo) — AFI credits Jack Nicholson as R. P. (Randle Patrick) McMurphy, Louise Fletcher as Nurse Ratched, Danny DeVito as Martini, and Will Sampson as Chief Bromden. It identifies the adaptation chain: Dale Wasserman’s play was based on Ken Kesey’s 1962 novel. AFI also records the 1975 shoot at Oregon State Hospital and credits Jack Nitzsche for original music.
2. [Academy: The 48th Academy Awards (1976)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1976) — the official ledger records nine nominations and five wins: Actor (Nicholson), Actress (Fletcher), directing (Milos Forman), Best Picture, and adapted screenplay (Lawrence Hauben and Bo Goldman). It also lists nominations for Brad Dourif, cinematography, editing, and original score.

**Distractor and source caution:** Fletcher is Nurse Ratched, DeVito is Martini, and Sampson is Chief Bromden; Nicholson plays McMurphy. The fictional psychiatric-hospital setting does not support medical claims about real mental-health care or diagnoses. The 1975 release and the 1976 Academy ceremony are distinct.
