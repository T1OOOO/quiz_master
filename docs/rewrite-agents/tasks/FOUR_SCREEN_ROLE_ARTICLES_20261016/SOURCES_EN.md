## 035 — *The Shawshank Redemption* (1994): Tim Robbins — Andy Dufresne

1. [AFI Catalog: *The Shawshank Redemption* (1994)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/55199-THE-SHAWSHANK-REDEMPTION) — AFI credits Tim Robbins as Andy Dufresne, Morgan Freeman as Ellis Boyd “Red” Redding, Bob Gunton as Warden Samuel Norton, and William Sadler as Heywood. It credits Frank Darabont as writer and director, Roger Deakins as cinematographer, Thomas Newman for music, and identifies the literary source as Stephen King’s 1982 novella “Rita Hayworth and the Shawshank Redemption.”
2. [Academy: The 67th Academy Awards (1995)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1995) — the official ledger records seven nominations: Morgan Freeman in Actor in a Leading Role, Roger Deakins for cinematography, Richard Francis-Bruce for editing, Thomas Newman for score, Best Picture, Sound, and Darabont’s adapted screenplay.

**Distractor and source caution:** Freeman plays Red, Gunton the warden, and Sadler Heywood; none plays Andy. The prison narrative is fiction adapted from King, not documentary evidence about real prisons. The film’s 1994 release and the 1995 Academy ceremony are separate dates.

## 036 — *The French Connection* (1971): Gene Hackman — Jimmy “Popeye” Doyle

1. [AFI Catalog: *The French Connection* (1971)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/53919-THE-FRENCH-CONNECTION?cxt=ymal) — AFI credits Gene Hackman as Jimmy “Popeye” Doyle, Fernando Rey as Alain Charnier, Roy Scheider as Buddy “Cloudy” Russo, and Tony Lo Bianco as Sal Boca. It lists Don Ellis as music composer and conductor and records the 1975 sequel, again with Hackman and Rey, while identifying the 1971 film as a drama/detective work.
2. [Academy: The 44th Academy Awards (1972)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1972) — the official ledger records eight nominations and five wins: Actor for Hackman, directing for William Friedkin, editing for Jerry Greenberg, Best Picture, and adapted screenplay for Ernest Tidyman. Scheider was nominated in supporting actor, with further nominations for Owen Roizman’s cinematography and sound.

**Distractor and source caution:** Rey is Charnier, Scheider is Russo, and Lo Bianco is Boca; the role in the question is Hackman’s Doyle. The film’s fictionalized screen story is not a source for claims about real police conduct. Its 1971 release precedes the 1972 ceremony.

## 037 — *Midnight Cowboy* (1969): Jon Voight — Joe Buck

1. [AFI Catalog: *Midnight Cowboy* (1969)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/23889-) — AFI credits Jon Voight as Joe Buck, Dustin Hoffman as Enrico “Ratso” Rizzo, Sylvia Miles as Cass, and Brenda Vaccaro as Shirley. The film adapts James Leo Herlihy’s novel; AFI records improvisational rehearsal and dialogue exercises by Voight and Hoffman, and notes it opened in 1969 with an X rating before its 1971 re-rating to R.
2. [Academy: The 42nd Academy Awards (1970)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1970) — the ledger records seven nominations and three wins: Best Picture, directing for John Schlesinger, and adapted screenplay for Waldo Salt. It lists both Hoffman and Voight as Actor nominees, Sylvia Miles as a supporting-actress nominee, and Hugh A. Robertson for editing.

**Distractor and source caution:** Hoffman plays Ratso, Miles plays Cass, and Vaccaro plays Shirley; Voight plays Joe Buck. The 1969 release, 1970 Academy ceremony, and 1971 rating change must not be collapsed into one date; the characters and their experiences are fictional.

## 038 — *Born on the Fourth of July* (1989): Tom Cruise — Ron Kovic

1. [AFI Catalog: *Born on the Fourth of July* (1989)](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/57927) — AFI credits Tom Cruise as Ron Kovic, Willem Dafoe as Charlie, and Kyra Sedgwick as Donna. It credits Oliver Stone and Ron Kovic as writers and identifies the source as Kovic’s 1976 autobiography. AFI records that Stone and Kovic collaborated on the screenplay and that Cruise’s casting was formally announced in 1988.
2. [Academy: The 62nd Academy Awards (1990)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1990) — the ledger records eight nominations and two wins, for directing (Stone) and film editing (David Brenner and Joe Hutshing). It lists Cruise as an Actor in a Leading Role nominee and identifies the adapted-screenplay nomination for Stone and Kovic.

**Distractor and source caution:** Dafoe plays Charlie and Sedgwick plays Donna; Pacino was associated with an earlier unmade version according to AFI, not the 1989 finished film. The film is an adaptation of Kovic’s autobiography and uses dramatic reconstruction; distinguish claims about the real Kovic from the film’s scenes. The release year is 1989 and the Academy ceremony year is 1990.
