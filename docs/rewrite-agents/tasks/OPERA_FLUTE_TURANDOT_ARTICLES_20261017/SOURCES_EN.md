## 009 — *The Magic Flute* (*Die Zauberflöte*): Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

1. [Salzburg Mozarteum Foundation: Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart](https://mozarteum.at/en/wolfgang-amade-mozart) — the Mozarteum dates the premiere to 30 September 1791 at Vienna’s Freihaus-Theater auf der Wieden, a suburban theatre leased to Emanuel Schikaneder. It classifies the work within the then-popular comic Singspiel repertoire and says Mozart conducted the first performances from the keyboard; Schikaneder played Papageno and Josepha Hofer sang the Queen of the Night.
2. [Digital Mozart Edition: *Die Zauberflöte* KV 620](https://dme.mozarteum.at/DME/libredition/single.php?end=8&idwnma=7137&line=0&v=421) — the critical edition identifies Wolfgang Amadé Mozart, calls the work a German opera in two acts, identifies Emanuel Schikaneder as author of the text, and records the first performance in Vienna on 30 September 1791. Its role list gives Tamino, Pamina, Sarastro, the Queen of the Night, and Papageno.

**Distractor and source caution:** Bizet, Verdi, and Puccini are opera composers but neither inspected Mozarteum record credits them for *The Magic Flute*; Mozart is the composer. The opera’s fairy-tale and Masonic motifs describe a fictional stage work, not historical evidence about ancient Egypt or Freemasonry.

## 010 — *Turandot*: Giacomo Puccini; Franco Alfano’s completion

1. [Giacomo Puccini Catalogue of the Works: *Turandot* notes](https://catalog.puccini.it/public/works/turandot/notes) — the catalogue traces the project to Carlo Gozzi’s 1762 drama and identifies Giuseppe Adami and Renato Simoni as the librettists. It documents that, after Puccini’s death, Franco Alfano was selected in summer 1925 to complete the work. It distinguishes the 25 April 1926 La Scala first performance, which stopped after Liù’s death without an Alfano ending, from the second and third performances, which used Alfano’s revised ending.
2. [Teatro alla Scala: *Turandot*, 2025–26 season](https://www.teatroallascala.org/en/season/2025-2026/opera/turandot/index.html) — La Scala credits music to Giacomo Puccini, the libretto to Giuseppe Adami and Renato Simoni, and the third-act completion to Franco Alfano. It calls the work an opera in three acts and five scenes and dates its world premiere at La Scala to 25 April 1926.

**Distractor and source caution:** Bizet, Mozart, and Verdi are not credited by either inspected source for *Turandot*; Puccini is the composer named in the question. Alfano completed material after Puccini’s death, but the inspected records do not make Alfano the work’s principal composer. Do not blur the unfinished score, Alfano’s two versions, the first performance without an Alfano ending, and subsequent performances with the revised ending.
