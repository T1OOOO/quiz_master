## 029 — *The Untouchables* (1987): Kevin Costner — Eliot Ness

1. [AFI Catalog: *The Untouchables* (1987)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/57873-THE-UNTOUCHABLES) — AFI credits Kevin Costner as Eliot Ness, with Brian De Palma directing and David Mamet writing. It identifies the film as suggested by the 1959–63 television series and books connected to Ness, and says Mamet’s screenplay framed Ness differently from Robert Stack’s television portrayal. AFI also records that the onscreen Catherine Ness and young daughter were fictionalized, even though Ness was married to Edna Staley during the period depicted.
2. [Academy: The 60th Academy Awards (1988)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1988/B) — the official Academy ledger records four nominations and one win for *The Untouchables*: Sean Connery won Actor in a Supporting Role; the other nominations were Art Direction, Costume Design, and Ennio Morricone’s Original Score. The 1988 ceremony honors 1987 releases.

**Safe article material:** Costner plays a dramatized film version of the real Prohibition-era lawman Eliot Ness. AFI’s explicit fictionalization of the screen family gives a concrete reason not to present the film’s domestic or action scenes as a documentary account. Connery’s award was for Jim Malone, not for Costner’s Ness, and it should not be described as a Best Actor award.

## 030 — *The Princess Bride* (1987): Cary Elwes — Westley

1. [AFI Catalog: *The Princess Bride* (1987)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/57788-THE-PRINCESS-BRIDE?cxt=ymal) — AFI credits Cary Elwes as Westley, with Rob Reiner directing and William Goldman writing. It says Goldman wrote the 1973 novel and the screenplay himself; the film closely follows the novel while making selected name and plot-detail changes, including the “Pit of Despair” instead of the novel’s “Zoo of Death.”
2. [Academy: The 60th Academy Awards (1988)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1988/B) — the Academy’s official ledger records one nomination for *The Princess Bride*: Original Song, “Storybook Love,” with music and lyrics by Willy DeVille. This is a song nomination, not an acting award for Elwes or an award win.

**Safe article material:** Westley, Buttercup, Florin, the Dread Pirate Roberts, and the framing grandfather/grandson story are fictional elements. The AFI synopsis identifies Westley’s “As you wish” reply as part of the film’s romance and identifies the Man in Black as Westley; describe these as story facts, not history. The adaptation point is strong because Goldman authored both the source novel and the screenplay.
