## 019 — *West Side Story* (1961): Natalie Wood — Maria

1. [AFI Catalog: *West Side Story* (1961)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/23857-west-sidestory) — AFI credits Natalie Wood as Maria in the 1961 cast and says Marni Nixon dubbed Wood’s singing voice. It separately identifies Steven Spielberg’s 2021 remake, in which Rachel Zegler played Maria and Rita Moreno played Valentina. AFI also records the film’s ten Academy Awards and its New York/Goldwyn Studios production.
2. [Academy: *West Side Story* (1961)](https://www.oscars.org/events/west-side-story-1961) — the Academy calls Maria “Natalie Wood,” describes the Jets/Sharks conflict around her relationship with Tony, identifies the film as a version of the Broadway musical, and lists the Laurents/Bernstein/Sondheim/Robbins creative origins. It records ten Oscars, including Best Picture and Directing.

**Safe article material:** the answer concerns who appeared on screen as Maria in the 1961 film: Wood. Nixon’s contribution is a distinct singing-dubbing credit, not an on-screen acting credit. Keep the 1961 film separate from the 2021 adaptation, where Zegler played Maria.

## 020 — *Rebel Without a Cause* (1955): James Dean — Jim Stark

1. [AFI Catalog: *Rebel Without a Cause* (1955)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/51633-REBEL-WITHOUTACAUSE) — AFI’s cast table credits James Dean as Jim Stark. It describes the police-station scene’s split framing of Jim, Judy and Plato, says the released story spans twenty-four hours, and explains that the production moved away from the 1944 Lindner case-history source toward a story about teenage discontent and isolation.
2. [Academy collection: James Dean](https://www.oscars.org/collection-highlights/james-dean?page=1) — the Academy states Dean appeared in only three films before his 1955 death, including *Rebel without a Cause*, and its collection includes material on Nicholas Ray, Natalie Wood, Griffith Observatory, the red jacket, locations, and Production Code revisions. Use this page for collection context, not its imprecise “latter two films” awards sentence.
3. [Academy: The 28th Academy Awards (1956)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1956/M) and [The 29th Academy Awards (1957)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1957/K) — the official ledgers list Dean as an Actor nominee for *East of Eden* at the 1956 ceremony and *Giant* at the 1957 ceremony. The 1956 ledger lists *Rebel without a Cause* nominations for Sal Mineo, Natalie Wood and Nicholas Ray, not for Dean.

**Safe article material:** Jim is fictional. The title’s early connection to Lindner’s real case history is development history, while the completed film’s characters and plot must not be presented as that case. Use camera/framing and the compressed twenty-four-hour structure as film craft, avoiding a diagnosis of any character. Do not claim a Dean Oscar nomination for this film: the Academy ledgers identify his nominations with *East of Eden* and *Giant*.

## 021 — *Psycho* (1960): Anthony Perkins — Norman Bates

1. [AFI Catalog: *Psycho* (1960)](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/53260) — AFI’s cast table credits Anthony Perkins as Norman Bates. It identifies the film as based on Robert Bloch’s 1959 novel, notes Saul Bass’s divided-line title design, and says the film reshaped novel-Norman as younger and more vulnerable in conjunction with Perkins’s casting.
2. [Academy collection: Alfred Hitchcock](https://www.oscars.org/collection-highlights/alfred-hitchcock) — the Academy places *Psycho* in Hitchcock’s 1958–63 sequence and calls it his first turn to full-blooded horror; it says *Psycho* and *The Birds* pressed viewers’ nerves, censorship boundaries, and advertising practice.

**Safe article material:** Norman Bates is a fictional character. The AFI note that Bloch partly drew the novel’s Norman from Ed Gein concerns the novel’s source history; it does not make the film’s events a factual account of Gein. Avoid clinical labels or diagnoses beyond describing credited narrative and adaptation choices.

## 022 — *The Maltese Falcon* (1941): Humphrey Bogart — Sam Spade

1. [AFI Catalog: *The Maltese Falcon* (1941)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/27025-THE-MALTESE-FALCON) — AFI credits Humphrey Bogart as Samuel Spade, John Huston as director and screenwriter, and identifies Dashiell Hammett’s 1930 novel as the literary source. AFI records that Warner Bros. had already made 1931 and 1936 films from the Hammett story; the 1941 version is therefore a separate adaptation, not the first screen version.
2. [Academy press release: Bogart double bill](https://digitalcollections.oscars.org/digital/api/collection/p15759coll4/id/15625/download) — the Academy’s 1993 release describes Huston’s film as starring Bogart with Mary Astor, Sydney Greenstreet and Peter Lorre, and records Oscar nominations for Greenstreet (supporting actor) and Huston (screenplay).

**Safe article material:** identify the answer as Bogart’s role in the 1941 film. Treat the falcon legend and the detective plot as fiction within an adaptation of Hammett’s novel; do not claim that the object is a historical artifact. Do not merge the 1941 version with Warner’s 1931 or 1936 adaptations.
