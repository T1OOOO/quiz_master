## 023 — *The African Queen* (1951): Katharine Hepburn — Rose Sayer

1. [AFI Catalog: *The African Queen* (1951)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/50007-THE-AFRICAN-QUEEN) — AFI credits Katharine Hepburn as Rose Sayer and identifies the film as based on C. S. Forester’s 1935 novel. Its synopsis sets Rose and Charlie Allnut on the river in German East Africa in 1914, then has Rose devise an attack on the German steamer *Louisa*. AFI records that close-ups on the small launch sometimes required reproducing parts of the boat on a raft.
2. [Academy: The 24th Academy Awards (1952)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1952) — the official ledger honors 1951 releases. It lists Humphrey Bogart as Actor winner for *The African Queen* and Katharine Hepburn as an Actress nominee; it also lists John Huston for Directing and James Agee and Huston for Screenplay nominations.

**Safe article material:** Rose is a fictional character in a literary adaptation. The 1914 East African setting and German vessel belong to the film’s narrative; they should not be described as a reconstruction of a specific historical voyage or attack. Hepburn was nominated for this performance, not the Actress winner at the 1952 ceremony.

## 024 — *Sunset Blvd.* / *Sunset Boulevard* (1950): Gloria Swanson — Norma Desmond

1. [AFI Catalog: *Sunset Blvd.* (1950)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/26513-SUNSET-BLVD) — AFI credits Gloria Swanson as Norma Desmond. It explains the opening’s unusual post-mortem narration by William Holden’s Joe Gillis and reports that an earlier morgue opening was cut after preview audiences laughed. AFI also documents the self-referential use of Swanson’s *Queen Kelly* footage, the return pairing with Erich von Stroheim, and silent-era figures including Cecil B. DeMille and Buster Keaton.
2. [Academy: The 23rd Academy Awards (1951)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1951) — the official ledger honors 1950 releases. It lists Gloria Swanson as Actress nominee and *Sunset Blvd.* with eleven nominations and three wins: black-and-white art direction, score for Franz Waxman, and original story/screenplay for Charles Brackett, Billy Wilder and D. M. Marshman Jr.

**Safe article material:** Norma Desmond is fictional, even though the film uses real Hollywood figures and imagery. AFI notes the contemporary title-card spelling “Sunset Blvd.”; use the quiz’s supplied Russian title and year to avoid conflating this film with later stage adaptations. Swanson was nominated, not the Actress winner at the 1951 ceremony.
