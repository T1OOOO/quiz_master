## 049 — Il trovatore: Giuseppe Verdi

1. [Teatro La Fenice: Il trovatore](https://www.teatrolafenice.it/en/event/trovatore_in_scenic_form/) — identifies Giuseppe Verdi as composer; dates the Rome premiere to 1853 and names the principal dramatic figures.
2. [Archivio Storico del Teatro dell’Opera di Roma: Il Trovatore](https://archiviostorico.operaroma.it/opera/il-trovatore/) — identifies Verdi, Cammarano, the four-part form, the Teatro Apollo absolute premiere on 19 January 1853, the principal characters, and Gutiérrez’s El trovador as literary source.
3. [Teatro La Fenice programme: Il trovatore](https://www.teatrolafenice.it/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/TROVATORE-IL.pdf) — identifies Verdi’s source as Antonio García Gutiérrez’s El trovador (Madrid, 1836); says Cammarano died in July 1852 and Leone Emanuele Bardare then made requested additions and changes. It dates the first performance to Rome’s Teatro Apollo, 19 January 1853, and explains the related but initially unrecognized relationship of Manrico and the Count di Luna.

**Caution:** chronology within the plot and chronology of composition are different questions. The characters and the revelation about their relationship belong to fiction, not historical biography.

## 050 — Tosca: Giacomo Puccini

1. [Puccini Museum: Tosca](https://www.puccinimuseum.org/giacomo-puccini/le-opere/tosca/) — identifies a three-act melodrama by Puccini on a libretto by Giuseppe Giacosa and Luigi Illica from Victorien Sardou’s play, with first performance at Rome’s Teatro Costanzi on 14 January 1900. The museum records Puccini’s encounter with Sardou’s play in 1889 and sustained composition/orchestration from 1898.
2. [Archivio Storico del Teatro dell’Opera di Roma: Tosca](https://archiviostorico.operaroma.it/opera/tosca/) — identifies Puccini, the composition years 1896–99, the original Italian, the Costanzi first performance, and Sardou as literary source.
3. [Teatro dell’Opera di Roma: Tosca](https://www.operaroma.it/spettacoli/opera-tosca-2015/) — gives the three stage locations: Sant’Andrea della Valle in Act I, Palazzo Farnese in Act II, and Castel Sant’Angelo in Act III, placing the fictional action in Rome in June 1800.

**Caution:** Rome’s landmarks and the 1800 setting support the fictional action’s geography, while 14 January 1900 is the work’s premiere date. Neither is a claim that the opera’s fictional events are historical record.
