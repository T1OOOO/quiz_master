## 027 — *Goodfellas* (1990): Robert De Niro — James “Jimmy the Gent” Conway

1. [AFI Catalog: *Goodfellas* (1990)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/55206-GOODFELLAS?cp=1&cxt=Filmography2&pos=6&sid=b958cc07-2e2b-4e6c-ab67-9b125e6d8a1b&sr=8.699503e-7) — AFI credits Robert De Niro as James “Jimmy the Gent” Conway. It records that the film was adapted from Nicholas Pileggi’s book *Wiseguy*, whose working title was changed to avoid confusion with the television drama *Wiseguy*. AFI also says De Niro recommended himself for the Conway supporting role, and describes the three-minute Copacabana tracking shot’s altered pathway through the nightclub.
2. [Academy: The 63rd Academy Awards (1991)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1991) — the official Academy ledger records six nominations and one win for *Good Fellas*: Joe Pesci won Actor in a Supporting Role; Lorraine Bracco, Martin Scorsese, Thelma Schoonmaker, Irwin Winkler, Nicholas Pileggi, and Martin Scorsese are listed in the remaining film nominations. It distinguishes this 1991 ceremony from the film’s 1990 release.

**Safe article material:** Jimmy Conway is a screen character in a dramatic adaptation, even though AFI reports that the film identifies itself as based on a true story. The Academy result belongs to Pesci and the film’s production team; it is not an acting award for De Niro, who played Conway. Avoid presenting the fictionalized plot or end-card information as a complete historical record.

## 028 — *Scarface* (1983): Al Pacino — Tony Montana

1. [AFI Catalog: *Scarface* (1983)](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/68337-SCARFACE?cxt=filmography) — AFI credits Al Pacino as Tony Montana, with Brian De Palma directing and Oliver Stone writing. It records that the 1983 film reworks the 1932 film’s “The World Is Yours” motif for Montana, that its setting is Miami, and that the finished film includes a disclaimer saying its fictional characters do not represent the Cuban-American community. AFI also records the rating appeal that changed the MPAA classification from X to R.
2. [AFI Catalog: *Scarface* (1932)](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/1134) — AFI identifies the earlier film as a 1932 Howard Hawks film with Paul Muni, based on Armitage Trail’s 1930 novel. Its history explicitly says Universal released another *Scarface* in 1983, directed by De Palma, written by Stone, and starring Pacino and others. This directly establishes that Pacino’s Tony Montana belongs to the 1983 remake, rather than to the 1932 film’s Tony Camonte.

**Safe article material:** Tony Montana is fictional; the 1983 credits themselves describe the story as fictional and reject treating its characters as representatives of Cuban Americans. The 1932 film, its novel source, and the 1983 remake are related adaptations, but Tony Camonte and Tony Montana are separate screen characters. Do not turn the Miami setting, Mariel-era prologue, or crime narrative into a documentary claim about real people or communities.
