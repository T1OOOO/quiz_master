# Cinema character articles: source brief

Research completed 2026-10-16 for the four current public questions. All listed pages were opened directly. These are research notes for original article drafting, not a publication or acceptance record.

## prep-film-actors2-007 — *Forrest Gump* (1994)

- **Credit:** Tom Hanks is credited as Forrest Gump. AFI also credits Robin Wright as Jenny Curran, Gary Sinise as Lieutenant Dan Taylor, and Mykelti Williamson as Bubba Blue.
- **Story / setting:** The film frames Forrest telling his life story from a bench in Savannah; use the film's fictional framing rather than presenting its historical encounters as documentary history.
- **Craft / context:** AFI records digitally added ping-pong balls and the specially designed wheelchair illusion used for Lieutenant Dan. The Academy records wins for Best Picture, Hanks's leading-actor performance, directing, editing, visual effects and adapted screenplay.
- **Sources opened:** AFI Catalog, *Forrest Gump*; Academy, *The 67th Academy Awards Memorable Moments*.

## prep-film-actors2-008 — *The Shining* (1980)

- **Credit:** Jack Nicholson is credited as Jack Torrance; Shelley Duvall as Wendy Torrance and Danny Lloyd as Danny.
- **Story / setting:** This is the 1980 Stanley Kubrick feature adaptation, not the later television adaptation. Jack takes the winter-caretaker job at the isolated Overlook Hotel, where the family is cut off for months; Danny's psychic ability is called “the shining.”
- **Craft / context:** AFI identifies Kubrick and Diane Johnson as the credited writers and records a final epilogue cut after preview screenings. Describe this as a production/editing decision, not proof of a single intended interpretation of the supernatural events.
- **Sources opened:** AFI Catalog, *The Shining*; Stephen King official works page, *The Shining*.

## prep-film-actors2-009 — *E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial* (1982)

- **Credit:** Henry Thomas plays Elliott. The Academy also identifies the director as Steven Spielberg and the screenplay writer as Melissa Mathison.
- **Story / setting:** Elliott befriends a stranded alien trying to return home. That relationship is the safest premise to foreground; it avoids reducing the film to a slogan or treating its fantasy events as real history.
- **Craft / context:** The Academy describes E.T.'s voice design and animatronic movement as collaborative technical work, and records four wins: original score, sound, sound-effects editing and visual effects. AFI documents the working titles *A Boy’s Life* and *E.T. and Me*, plus the three soundstage setup that separated the family rooms, house/backyard and forest landing set.
- **Sources opened:** AFI Catalog, *E.T.: The Extra-Terrestrial*; Academy, *“E.T. The Extra-Terrestrial”* (Prime Tech event).

## prep-film-actors2-010 — *Black Panther* (2018)

- **Credit:** Chadwick Boseman plays T’Challa / Black Panther, the king of the fictional nation Wakanda. Do not confuse Boseman’s 2018 film role with the comic character or later sequel.
- **Story / setting:** BFI describes Wakanda as a fictional, self-sufficient kingdom and identifies Erik Killmonger and Ulysses Klaue as threats to T’Challa’s reign. The Academy's synopsis likewise calls T’Challa newly crowned and Wakanda technologically advanced but hidden from the outside world.
- **Craft / context:** The Academy explains Ruth E. Carter's costume approach as Afrofuturist, combining African mythologies and science fiction, handcrafted and 3D-printed accessories, and traditional styles with modern silhouettes. In a BFI interview, Ryan Coogler says the ancient Mali empire influenced Wakanda's tribal design and that the team wanted Wakanda to feel like a real place built over a long time.
- **Sources opened:** Academy, *The Art of Costume Design: Black Panther (2018)*; BFI film record, *Black Panther (2018)*; BFI interview, *Black Panther and a black Bond? Ryan Coogler on his favourite film and influences*.

## Limits carried into drafting

- Treat awards as institutional records rather than a measure of artistic truth.
- Separate the films' fictional worlds and historical references from real-world history.
- Do not claim that *The Shining*'s ambiguities have a single official explanation.
- Marvel pages found in search were opened but returned 403 through the research fetcher, so they are deliberately omitted from the usable source set.
