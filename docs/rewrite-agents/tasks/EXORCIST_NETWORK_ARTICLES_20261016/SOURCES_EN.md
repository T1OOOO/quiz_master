## 031 — *The Exorcist* (1973): Ellen Burstyn — Chris MacNeil

1. [AFI Catalog: *The Exorcist* (1973)](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/54970) — AFI credits Ellen Burstyn as Chris MacNeil and William Friedkin as director; William Peter Blatty is credited as screenwriter and producer, based on his own novel. AFI documents production choices: the set used sub-zero air so actors’ breath would show, and the sound crew recorded live-source effects including animals, a convulsing woman, and a trapped bee. It also distinguishes the 2000 DVD director’s cut from the original theatrical release.
2. [Academy: The 46th Academy Awards (1974)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1974) — the official ledger honors 1973 releases and records ten nominations and two wins for *The Exorcist*: Sound (Robert Knudson and Chris Newman) and Adapted Screenplay (Blatty). Burstyn was an Actress nominee for the film; Linda Blair and Jason Miller were supporting-acting nominees.

**Safe article material:** Chris MacNeil, Regan, and the possession narrative are fictional dramatic characters and events. The film’s religious imagery or plot does not establish medical, psychiatric, or religious facts. The Academy outcomes support the exact categories and people, while AFI’s sound and production material supports craft discussion; do not use AFI’s Linda Blair “film debut” or Max von Sydow age assertions, which require separate verification.

## 032 — *Network* (1976): Peter Finch — Howard Beale

1. [AFI Catalog: *Network* (1976)](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/54924) — AFI credits Peter Finch as Howard Beale, with Sidney Lumet directing and Paddy Chayefsky writing. It records the film’s four major Academy wins and identifies the widely promoted “I’m as mad as hell” slogan as part of the film’s advertising campaign. AFI says CBS later acquired television rights and edited material for broadcast, a useful example of the film’s subsequent television life.
2. [Academy: The 49th Academy Awards (1977)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1977) — the official ledger records ten nominations and four wins for *Network*: Peter Finch won Actor in a Leading Role; Faye Dunaway, Beatrice Straight, and Paddy Chayefsky also won in their listed categories. It records the additional nominations for William Holden, Ned Beatty, Owen Roizman, Sidney Lumet, and Alan Heim.

**Safe article material:** Howard Beale is a fictional television anchor in a comedy-drama, not an actual broadcaster or case study. The award is Finch’s Actor in a Leading Role win for his performance, while the slogan is part of the film and its promotion; neither supports claims about real television audiences or journalism as a whole.
