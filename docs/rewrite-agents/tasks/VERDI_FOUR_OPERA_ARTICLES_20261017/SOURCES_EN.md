## 045 — *La traviata*: Giuseppe Verdi

1. [Teatro La Fenice: *La traviata*](https://www.teatrolafenice.it/en/event/giuseppe-verdi-la-traviata/) identifies *La traviata* as a three-act opera by Giuseppe Verdi on an Italian libretto by Francesco Maria Piave. It traces the subject to Alexandre Dumas fils’s *La dame aux camélias* and dates the first performance at La Fenice, Venice, to 6 March 1853.
2. [Teatro La Fenice programme: *La traviata*, dal dramma alla musica](https://www.teatrolafenice.it/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/TRAVIATA-LA-2.pdf) records the original libretto as Piave’s and the music as Verdi’s. Its scholarly essay says the source drama was Dumas fils’s 1852 stage adaptation of his 1848 novel and documents how the opera condensed its five acts into a three-act structure.

**Caution:** Puccini, Rossini, and Bizet are opera composers but are not credited by these Fenice records for this work. La Fenice reports that its authorities required the first production to be set around 1700 rather than the contemporary setting wanted by Verdi and Piave; this is a local production-history claim, not a general claim about all later stagings.

## 046 — *Rigoletto*: Giuseppe Verdi

1. [Teatro La Fenice: *Rigoletto*](https://www.teatrolafenice.it/en/event/rigoletto/) calls the opera a 1850 La Fenice commission and the first of Verdi’s middle-period operas. The theatre says censors objected to aspects of its content on aesthetic and political grounds and describes the work’s dramatic continuity as moving away from a routine aria-plus-cabaletta pattern.
2. [Teatro La Fenice historical programme: *Rigoletto*](https://www.teatrolafenice.it/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Rigoletto_-light.pdf) reproduces the first-performance libretto title page, naming Francesco Maria Piave for the drama and Giuseppe Verdi for the music, and dates the absolute premiere at La Fenice to 11 March 1851. Its essay explicitly compares the opera’s changes with Victor Hugo’s source drama.

**Caution:** Rossini, Wagner, and Puccini are not credited by the inspected Fenice materials for *Rigoletto*. The source relationship is an adaptation context; its invented characters and events are not history.

## 047 — *Aida*: Giuseppe Verdi

1. [Teatro Regio di Parma: *Aida*](https://teatroregioparma.it/spettacolo/aida/) credits Giuseppe Verdi for the music and Antonio Ghislanzoni for the four-act libretto. It dates the first performance to the Khedivial Opera House in Cairo on 24 December 1871 and separately dates the Italian first performance at La Scala, Milan, to 8 February 1872.
2. [Archivio Storico del Teatro dell’Opera di Roma: *Aida*](https://archiviostorico.operaroma.it/opera/aida/) records Verdi as composer and Ghislanzoni as librettist, identifies an outline by Auguste Mariette reworked by Camille Du Locle with Verdi, and gives Cairo, 24 December 1871 as the absolute premiere.

**Caution:** Wagner, Puccini, and Rossini are not credited by the inspected records for *Aida*. Cairo in 1871 is the world premiere; the 1872 La Scala date is the Italian premiere, so neither should be substituted for the other. The story is fictional stage material and should not be described as a record of ancient Egyptian or Ethiopian history.

## 048 — *Nabucco* (*Nabucodonosor*): Giuseppe Verdi

1. [Teatro alla Scala: *Nabucodonosor*, 2025–26 season](https://www.teatroallascala.org/en/season/2025-2026/opera/nabucodonosor.html) identifies Giuseppe Verdi and calls the work a four-act *dramma lirico* on a libretto by Temistocle Solera. It describes the 1842 La Scala triumph as Verdi’s definitive breakthrough and says the 2026 production performs divertissements Verdi wrote for an 1848 Brussels revival.
2. [Teatro alla Scala: Chailly interview on *Nabucodonosor*](https://www.teatroallascala.org/it/media/la-scala-rivista-del-teatro/il-primo-capolavoro-intervista-riccardo-chailly.html) discusses the work’s biblical subject and Solera libretto, and points to distinct orchestral and choral writing, including six solo cellos in Zaccaria’s prayer and the choral writing in “Va, pensiero.”

**Caution:** Bizet, Rossini, and Puccini are not credited by the inspected La Scala records for *Nabucco*. La Scala links the 1842 success with Italian unification and the Risorgimento, but this is an institutional historical framing; it does not prove the simplified legend that “Va, pensiero” was an immediate national anthem or that every audience response had one political meaning.
