# Classic film articles: source brief

Research completed 2026-10-16 for the four current public actor-role questions. Every source below was opened directly. These notes support original drafting; they neither publish articles nor establish acceptance.

## prep-film-actors2-011 — *Singin’ in the Rain* (1952)

- **Credit:** Gene Kelly plays Don Lockwood. Donald O’Connor plays Cosmo Brown, Debbie Reynolds plays Kathy Selden, and Jean Hagen plays Lina Lamont.
- **Premise:** The fictional Hollywood story uses the industry’s transition to sound as its comic setting. Keep the plot’s studio-era transformation separate from a claim that it documents a particular real studio’s history.
- **Craft / history:** AFI says Comden and Green developed a story reflecting both comedy and the sadness of the sound transition. Most songs were reused from 1920s–30s MGM musicals; only “Make ’Em Laugh” and “Moses” were written especially for the film. The Academy records two nominations—Jean Hagen for supporting actress and Lennie Hayton for musical-picture scoring—so do not say that Kelly won an acting Oscar for this film.
- **Sources opened:** AFI Catalog, *Singin’ in the Rain*; Academy, *The 25th Academy Awards*.

## prep-film-actors2-012 — *Some Like It Hot* (1959)

- **Credit:** Marilyn Monroe plays Sugar Kane Kowalczyk; the film’s other central stars are Tony Curtis and Jack Lemmon.
- **Premise:** Two financially struggling musicians flee gangsters by disguising themselves as women and joining an all-female band travelling to Florida. This is fictional comic action, not an account of Depression-era musicians’ lives.
- **Craft / history:** AFI traces the plot to the 1951 German film *Fanfaren der Liebe* and notes Florida sequences were filmed at Hotel del Coronado near San Diego. The Academy identifies Wilder and I. A. L. Diamond as co-writers and records six nominations with one win, for Orry-Kelly’s black-and-white costume design; Jack Lemmon was nominated for actor.
- **Sources opened:** AFI Catalog, *Some Like It Hot*; Academy collection highlights, *Some Like It Hot*.

## prep-film-actors2-013 — *The Graduate* (1967)

- **Credit:** Dustin Hoffman plays Benjamin “Ben” Braddock; Anne Bancroft plays Mrs Robinson and Katharine Ross plays Elaine Robinson.
- **Premise:** Ben returns home after college, unsure of his future, becomes involved with Mrs Robinson, then falls for her daughter Elaine. Present this as a fictional character story, not advice or a documentary portrait of one generation.
- **Craft / history:** The film adapts Charles Webb’s 1963 novel. AFI documents Buck Henry’s late work on plot problems and his screen credit with Calder Willingham. The Academy describes Nichols’s Panavision framing and Simon & Garfunkel songs, and records Nichols’s directing win; Hoffman's performance was nominated, not victorious.
- **Sources opened:** AFI Catalog, *The Graduate*; Academy, *The Graduate / Point Blank*.

## prep-film-actors2-014 — *To Kill a Mockingbird* (1962)

- **Credit:** Gregory Peck plays Atticus Finch. The film is based on Harper Lee’s novel; Robert Duvall makes his screen debut as Boo Radley, while Mary Badham plays Scout.
- **Premise:** Set in a fictional Depression-era Alabama community, the story follows lawyer Atticus as he defends a Black man falsely accused of rape and is also seen through Scout’s coming-of-age perspective. Do not treat the fictional case as a real historical trial.
- **Craft / history:** AFI records that production designers moved period cottages from demolition sites to the Universal lot to build the setting; Peck said location filming was abandoned because Monroeville no longer resembled the novel’s Depression-era community. The Academy records Peck’s actor win, black-and-white art-direction win, and Horton Foote’s adapted-screenplay win.
- **Sources opened:** AFI Catalog, *To Kill a Mockingbird*; Academy, *“To Kill a Mockingbird”*.

## Limits carried into drafting

- Awards are institutional records, not a measure of artistic truth.
- Use the exact ceremony and category stated above; the award ceremony year follows the film release year.
- Explain fictional settings and events as film narrative rather than real history.
- These references concern the specified feature films, not later stage, television, or other adaptations.
