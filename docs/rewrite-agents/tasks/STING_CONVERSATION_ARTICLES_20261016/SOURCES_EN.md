## 033 — *The Sting* (1973): Paul Newman — Henry Gondorff

1. [AFI Catalog: *The Sting* (1973)](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/54391) — AFI credits Paul Newman as Henry Gondorff and Robert Redford as Johnny Hooker. Its synopsis describes Hooker being directed to Chicago to meet Gondorff for a “big con.” AFI also credits Marvin Hamlisch with adapting the score from Scott Joplin melodies; the main theme derives from Joplin’s 1902 “The Entertainer.”

Ward; the ledger also lists its cinematography and sound nominations.

## 034 — *The Conversation* (1974): Gene Hackman — Harry Caul

1. [AFI Catalog: *The Conversation* (1974)](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/53857) — AFI credits Gene Hackman as Harry Caul and Francis Ford Coppola as director, writer, and producer. It records that Coppola began the screenplay in 1966 and finished it in 1970. Its plot synopsis places the fictional story in San Francisco, where Caul and colleagues record a conversation in Union Square.
2. [Festival de Cannes: *The Conversation*](https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/f/the-conversation/) — the festival’s official record lists the film as a 1974 In Competition feature and records the **Grand Prix International du Festival 1974**. It credits Coppola for direction and screenplay, Bill Butler for cinematography, and David Shire for music; Gene Hackman is listed in the cast.
3. [Academy: The 47th Academy Awards (1975)](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1975) — the official ledger records three nominations: Best Picture (Francis Ford Coppola and Fred Roos), Sound (Walter Murch and Arthur Rochester), and Original Screenplay (Coppola).

**Safe article material:** Hackman plays the fictional Harry Caul. The 1974 film release, its 1974 Cannes recognition, and the Academy ceremony in 1975 are distinct dates. The Cannes source uses the historical award name “Grand Prix International du Festival”; this brief does not relabel it as the modern runner-up Grand Prix or retrospectively call it a different trophy. Surveillance is a fictional plot device here, not technical guidance or evidence about real surveillance practice.
