# P29 — family creative pilot and live-team brief

## Facts

- The planned first-release question interactions are deterministic single choice, multiple choice, and explicit normalized text; scored correctness is not AI-graded.
- Card, explanation, public/reveal payload separation, and the team extension remain contract work. In particular, immediate private correctness versus shared correctness reveal is explicitly pending.
- The live-team plan records proposals—not settled behavior—for one captain answering device per team, admission closed after start, host-authorized captain transfer, final/no-resubmission answers, and fixed correctness points with no speed bonus. It also describes concurrent authoritative submissions, private host arrivals, a public presenter, and a shared explanation/standings step; P33 must settle the listed proposals.
- The first pilot must work on Flutter Web and Android, including a projected presenter view, keyboard/touch operation, readable 4–6-option layouts, reload/reconnect behavior, and Russian/English-ready copy. P30 drafts; P31 independently solves and fact-checks; P32 validates the accepted revision and rendering.

## Assumptions

- The pilot is an editorial and interaction trial, not a newly published pack or a settled P33 contract.
- A ten-question round should fit roughly 12–15 minutes when the host permits 20–30 seconds to read each explanation and transition. Exact round timer and release behavior remain P33 decisions.
- Family-friendly means shared play across approximately ages 10+ and adults: curious rather than childish, no trick wording, no distressing subject matter, and no cultural knowledge required solely to understand an item.

## Recommendation

Use **“Everyday inventions: how familiar things work and where they came from”** for a ten-question pilot. Its tone is warm, observant, and collaborative: “you have seen this—now notice one surprising detail.” It suits families, avoids competitive specialist knowledge, supports short source-backed explanations, and gives a presenter a natural bridge between questions. It is also a useful live-round trial because answers can be discussed without revealing a personal or sensitive fact.

Recommended cognitive mix: six single-choice questions (recognition, simple cause/effect, or sequencing), two multiple-choice questions (select all true features; exactly one clearly defined set), and two normalized-text questions (a short named object/person/place with accepted spelling variants specified before validation). Sequence difficulty as easy, easy, medium, easy, medium, medium, easy, medium, medium, easy. Put a single-choice item first to establish mechanics; put the two text items after teams have seen the answer controls; reserve the final easy single choice for a confident finish. Keep every item answerable from a concise stem without image-only information, even if future cards add media.

For each question, the writer records a primary or otherwise authoritative source, the exact fact asserted, answer rationale, and a concise explanation that adds one useful “why” beyond the key. Distractors must be plausible to a reasonably informed player, same grammatical category and comparable length; they must not depend on a misleading qualifier, an obscure exception, or a joke. For multiple choice, state the selection rule and avoid near-duplicate options. For text, define Unicode/case/whitespace normalization and explicit accepted variants; do not rely on fuzzy matching.

P30 should draft the stems, options, explanations and source records. P31 should independently solve each item before seeing its key, verify the cited fact and flag ambiguity, harmful framing, weak distractors, or untestable text variants. P32 should confirm schema validity, supported type/card rendering, option count, public/private field separation, and localized strings. Accessibility review belongs in all three: plain language, no color-only cue, touch targets and keyboard order, pronounceable/expandable unfamiliar terms, and explanations that work when read aloud. Localization should preserve the fact rather than mechanically translate a pun; translators need source context and accepted text variants per locale.

Provisional planning default: teams immediately receive a committed submission acknowledgement, the host privately sees arrival/answer status, and correctness plus explanation are shared only after the host closes the question. The associated one-device-per-team, closed-admission-after-start, host-authorized captain-transfer, final/no-resubmission, and no-speed-bonus defaults are also proposals pending P33, not final user approval. P33 must preserve an explicit configurable reveal policy if the later decision requires immediate private correctness.

### Observable acceptance scenario

A presenter starts this ten-question round and, under the provisional P33 defaults, admits two teams, “Comets” and “Sparks,” each with one captain phone; admission closes when play starts and the host alone may authorize a captain transfer. Both phones and the projector show question one; Comets submit a single choice, receive an accepted receipt, and the host privately sees Comets as answered without exposing their option. Sparks submit before the server deadline and receive the same kind of receipt. Under the proposed final/no-resubmission policy, each team has one accepted answer and retries remain idempotent rather than double-score; P33 must settle any editing/resubmission rule. At close, the presenter reveals the correct answer and a 20–30 second explanation to both teams, then shows standings and opens the next question. The same pattern works for multiple choice and normalized text: no phone sees correctness before shared reveal under the provisional default, and no speed bonus applies. If P33 selects immediate private correctness instead, the scenario must change only that team-private outcome while preserving public secrecy until shared reveal.

### Alternatives

1. **“Nature in the neighbourhood.”** Strong family appeal and easy visual follow-up, but regional species and seasonal assumptions increase localization/fact-check cost.
2. **“Food science in the kitchen.”** Highly conversational and concrete, but allergy, dietary culture, safety advice, and disputed folk claims require tighter editorial filtering.

This is advisory only; it does not freeze scoring (including no speed bonus), reveal timing, one-device-per-team, final/no-resubmission, captain transfer, admission-after-start, or release scope.
